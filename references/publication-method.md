# Publication and reviewer mode

## Manuscript spine

1. Current scientific question and evidence boundary.
2. Claims grouped `primary`, `supporting`, `interpretive` and `speculative`. A speculative statement is not a verified result.
3. Figure map: every figure has a question, registered analysis source, primary quantity, uncertainty and alternative reading.
4. Sections: motivation → data and provenance → method and controls → results and failures → discussion with competing explanations → limits → concise conclusions.
5. Keep the author's actual text, terminology and paragraph structure where practical; avoid unnecessary jargon and repeatedly changing labels.

## Sentence-level claim audit

For each factual sentence: claim ID → experiment ID → data/result filename → variable/row → uncertainty → figure/table → citation source and supported passage. A bibliography DOI can be real while its link to the assertion is unsupported; check both.

## Review response requirements

- Reproduce each reviewer concern accurately; classify as resolved by existing results, needs a minimal new experiment, needs clarification, or outside current evidence.
- When proposing a new experiment, return to the research mode and preregister it; do not change existing decision gates after seeing data.
- Explain precisely the manuscript location changed; keep a redline or change list.
- Do not promise datasets/code availability unless they exist and sharing is permitted.
- Use `cannot conclude` or `not distinguishable under this setup` when evidence is insufficient; absence of recoverability is not nonexistence.

## Figures

Maps intended for direct comparison need matched extent, projection, units, spectrum, uncertainty and a defensible color scale. Record smoothing and visualization choices separately from numerical model changes. No data-less decorative fill for a scientific result figure. Visual schematics must be clearly labeled schematic.
